Serolsar Lake trek guide: distance, difficulty and best time

Everything you need before walking from Jalori Pass to the sacred lake.

Call +91 70187 36861WhatsApp

The Serolsar Lake trek is a short, gentle forest walk from Jalori Pass to a calm, sacred lake in the Seraj region of Himachal Pradesh. It is one of the easiest treks in the area, which is why families, couples and first-time trekkers choose it. This guide covers the distance, the difficulty, the best months and how to get to the start.

By Raghupur Fort Camps • Updated 20 Nov 2026

Serolsar Lake trek at a glance

  • Distance: about 5 km one way from Jalori Pass.
  • Time: roughly 2 hours one way at an easy pace. Add time for photos and rest.
  • Altitude: the lake is at about 3,100 m (around 10,170 ft).
  • Difficulty: easy. The trail is mostly a gradual forest path.
  • Starting point: Jalori Pass, near the Kali Mata temple.
  • Best months: April to November. In winter it becomes a snow trek.

These figures are approximate and the trail time depends on your group, the weather and the trail condition on the day.

How to reach the Serolsar Lake trailhead

The trek begins at Jalori Pass, which is about 12 km above Jibhi. From Delhi or Chandigarh most travellers go via Mandi and the Aut tunnel, then Banjar and Jibhi, and take a local taxi up to the pass. The last stretch is a steep mountain road. Our how to reach Jalori Pass page lists the bus, road and airport options in full.

How difficult is the Serolsar Lake trek?

Most people find it easy. There are no steep climbs, but remember that you are walking above 3,000 m, so the air is thinner than in the plains. A few things to plan for:

  • Walk slowly and drink water, especially if you came up from Jibhi the same morning.
  • The forest floor can be slippery after rain, so wear shoes with grip.
  • Weather in the mountains turns fast. Start early and carry a rain layer.
  • Mobile signal is patchy in the forest, so tell someone your plan.

Best time to visit Serolsar Lake

April to June brings mild days and rhododendron bloom along the trail. July and August are monsoon months with heavy rain and landslide risk on the road, so check conditions first. September to November usually gives crisp air and clear views. From late December to early March snow can close the Jalori road, and the trek is only for those prepared for snow. Read more in our Jalori Pass winter and monsoon road guide.

What to carry

  • Water bottle and light snacks
  • A warm layer and a waterproof jacket
  • Trekking shoes with good grip
  • Sunscreen, cap and sunglasses
  • A small bag to carry your waste back out

The full list is on our trip planning page.

Lake etiquette

Serolsar is a holy lake, with a small temple to Budhi Nagin on its bank. Please do not swim or wash in the water, remove your shoes near the shrine and carry all plastic back to Jalori Pass. Visitors who keep the place clean help keep it that way.

Make it a day trek or a camp night

You can walk to the lake as a guided day trek, or turn it into two days with a night in a tent on the Raghupur Fort ridge. See the Serolsar Lake trek and camping packages or the Raghupur Fort camp.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Serolsar Lake trek?

It is about 5 km one way from Jalori Pass and usually takes around 2 hours each way at an easy pace.

Is the Serolsar Lake trek difficult?

No. It is generally rated easy and is a popular first trek, but the altitude is above 3,000 m, so walk slowly and carry water.

What is the best time for the Serolsar Lake trek?

April to June and September to November are the most comfortable months. Monsoon (July to August) brings rain and landslide risk, and winter brings snow.

Can I swim in Serolsar Lake?

No. The lake is considered sacred, and visitors are asked not to enter or wash in the water.
